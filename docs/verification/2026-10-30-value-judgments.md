# Judged deck-building sessions, 2026-10-30

What this answers: are the server's deck-building ideas worth having, measured the same way three times across the day's fixes. Each session is a verbatim run through the public endpoint of a builder making a Cad Bane, Still Faster than You / Nevarro City, Restored deck against the current Premier meta, scored step by step by an adversarial judge (opus, no stake, same rubric each time: 0 misleading or empty, 1 correct but nothing new, 2 would influence a real card choice, 3 a non-obvious idea a strong builder acts on).

## TL;DR

Mean score moved from 1.09 to 1.25 to 1.50 to 1.64 to 1.57 to 1.57 to 1.71 of 3 across seven builds on the Cad Bane deck, then to 2.29 in the last of ten reruns on 2026-10-31 and 2026-11-01 (1.79, 1.86, 1.93, 2.07, 2.21, 2.21, 2.21, 2.14, 2.21, 2.29). On the owner's own Warrior deck (session 8) it moved from 1.00 to 2.21 across eighteen runs, with no step at 0 from run F on. The share of steps a builder would act on (score 2 or 3) moved from 2 of 11 to 14 of 14 on the Cad Bane deck and from 3 of 14 to 10 of 14 on the Warrior deck. The tournament-data tools (meta records, leader-ranked lists, `leader_cards` lift) earn their keep. Both sessions have now scored VALUABLE on six consecutive runs (Warrior runs M to R, Cad Bane reruns w20 to w25, waves 20 to 25: #166 to #170 and #173), seven counting wave 19 (#165). The latest are Warrior 2.21 (31 of 42, 10 of 14 steps at 2 or above) and Cad Bane 2.29 (32 of 42, 14 of 14 steps at 2 or above) (judge-8-w25.md and judge-7-w25.md, session scratchpad, not committed). The Warrior judge says steps 1, 2, 10 and 11 stay at 1 because of data, the harness or the question itself. The margins are thin and the Warrior verdict rests on calibration. Across these six waves the Warrior judge's blind totals ran 22 to 27 of 42 (1.57 to 1.93, MIXED on its own scale in every run) and the Cad Bane judge's ran 29 to 31 (2.07 to 2.21, VALUABLE on its own scale). The calibrated totals the verdicts use ran 29 to 31 for the Warrior deck and 30 to 32 for the Cad Bane deck. A calibrated total is diff-anchored: a step whose output is byte-identical to the previous run keeps the previous run's score unless the judge finds a clear error, and a changed step is scored on whether the previous run's named defect is fixed, as each report states. The Warrior gap between calibrated and blind totals ran 3, 5, 3, 6, 9 and 7 points across runs M to R, against 0 to 2 points on the Cad Bane deck, so part of the Warrior upward trend is the anchor and not a fresh scoring. The Warrior judge puts the next ceiling at 32 of 42 (2.29) and needs one harness change (step 8 passing the 50-card list); the Cad Bane judge puts a realistic ceiling at about 35 of 42 (2.50). The sections below keep each run's reasons.
