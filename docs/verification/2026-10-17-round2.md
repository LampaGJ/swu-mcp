# Accuracy re-measure after fixes (round 2)

## TL;DR

The fixes for #10 to #13 repaired every defect found in round 1, and each now has a regression test. A fresh, independently seeded sample shows the overall error rate did not move: 47 of 292 items wrong (16.1%), against 46 of 292 (15.8%) in round 1. The errors have a long tail. Each new sample finds new phrasings and new qualifiers. Copying from the originals is still exact: the deterministic audit passes with 0 failures. Card text, rulings, prices, play stats and rules sections can be trusted. Pairings, trios and fungible classes are candidates for a person or a model to confirm, not facts.

## Method

- Same packet types and builder as round 1 (`pnpm verify:sample`), new seed `verify-round2`, drawn after #10 to #13 landed and the data was rebuilt.
- Eight haiku agents judged the packets. Two sonnet agents then adjudicated every "wrong" and "unsure" flag against the original sources, and spot-checked every 10th "correct" item.
- Rates below are confirmed-wrong over judged items. Still-unsure items are excluded. Intervals are Wilson 95%.

## Results, round 1 then round 2

- Card-text features (two packets combined): 20/80 = 25.0% (16.8 to 35.5), then 17/79 = 21.5% (13.9 to 31.8).
- Curated entries: 2/39 = 5.1%, then 3/39 = 7.7% (2.7 to 20.3).
- Pairings: 14/39 = 35.9% (22.7 to 51.6), then 17/39 = 43.6% (29.3 to 59.0).
- Fungible classes: 4/25 = 16.0%, then 7/25 = 28.0% (14.3 to 47.6).
- Rules sections: 0/40, then 0/40 (upper bound 8.8%).
- Glossary: 3/39 = 7.7%, then 1/40 = 2.5% (0.4 to 12.9).
- Field guide: 3/30 = 10.0%, then 2/30 = 6.7% (1.8 to 21.3).
- All packets: 46/292 = 15.8% (12.0 to 20.4), then 47/292 = 16.1% (12.3 to 20.7).

No change is statistically clear at these sample sizes. Haiku raised 108 flags, and adjudication found 63 of them were haiku's own errors. Single-model judging alone is not a usable measure here.

## Root causes found in round 2

- Pairing ignores a qualifier (RC06, 13): cost limits, trait and side restrictions, and tags such as give-weakness and give-shield whose polarity the matcher does not check. Locations: `src/synergy/qualifiers.ts`, `src/ability-grammar/tags.ts`.
- Curated entry models its clause wrongly (RC19, 10): cur-0003, 0060, 0063, 0073, 0074, 0080, 0107, 0151, 0153. All are in `data/curated-rules.json`.
- Condition left as residue (RC13, 8), including the "you've" contraction, which the rule at `src/ability-grammar/grammar.ts:316` never matches.
- Side not taken from an ownership phrase (new, 4): "you play" and "its controller's base". `parseTarget` sets side `any`.
- A rule drops its qualifier (RC14, 3): the Fortify restriction, `lose_keyword` keeping no keyword, and `look` keeping no target.
- Single cases: target kind chosen by regex order; "can't gain abilities" unmatched; "for this round" read as "for this phase"; `play_from` always n=1; indirect damage tagged as any-unit damage; an any-side base tagged as enemy; "non-unit" matching `\bunits?\b`; a discard cost tagged as a discard payoff; an upgrade's effect on its host ignored; a self-created discard read as an existing one.
- Docs: the Fortify strategic value is wrong (RC20). Two field-guide counts do not reproduce (RC21): deployBox is 515 not 513, and foil false is 5,004 not 4,969. Other field-guide counts may also be off.

## What this means

- A rule-based extractor reaches roughly 75 to 80% item accuracy on card text, and each fix closes a narrow slice of a long tail.
- Pairing accuracy stays near 55 to 65%, because a pair inherits both cards' tag errors plus the qualifier checks.
- The MCP tools should present pairings, trios and fungible substitutes as candidates. They should return both cards' text with each result, so the calling model or person can confirm each link. That keeps scoring deterministic while still catching errors.
- A labelled fixture of about 200 pairs, judged once and committed, would give a stable precision measure. Fixes could then be tested against it rather than against fresh samples.
